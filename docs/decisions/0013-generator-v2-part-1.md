# 0013. Generator v2, part 1: integer generation, four styles as data, extended mixes as regions, the legacy baseline

**Status:** Accepted, 2026-11-04 (S1b1). ADR 0023 (S1b2a, proposed) amends it for forms drawn per seed: it does §2's last two "Reserved for S1b2" bullets, replaces §4's one form per style (with its 6 to 7½ minutes and its region names alike for every seed of a kit) and adds `regions.json`'s one-bar dropouts. Its Consequences re-measure §3's hold-gap figures on the drawn forms (the rule still changes no shipped output) and give §7's new file count; the text below stays S1b1's record. ADR 0026 (S1b2b, the hook, accepted 2026-11-15) does §2's reserved per-seed motif for FUT02's arp and gives FUT01, FUT03 and FUT04 a lead lane; §2's reserved reese drop layer becomes its PR 3, FUT01's written warp riff and bass talk (planned).

## Context
S1b1 is the first half of generator v2 (`docs/plans/S1-futuristic-slice.md` §4, §5.1 and the S1b1 row of §7). It has to:
- generate MIDI that is the same bytes on every platform (CLAUDE.md, "Determinism and golden files");
- write MIDI in the idiom of current club music for each of the four kits' directions (`docs/plans/S1-four-kits.md`), never copying a record;
- deliver every seed as a DJ-friendly extended mix in MIDI regions, which the user asked for on top of the plan;
- prove itself against the legacy generator: frozen `core.js` must fail the gating metrics, and its baseline must never drift.

**Numbering.** The plan's §8.2 put this ADR at 0010. Numbers 0010 to 0012 went to S1a's decisions (four first-listen kits, the kick crest floor, samples in Git LFS), so this is 0013, the next free number. The plan's later numbers are stale in the same way: from now on an ADR takes the next free number when it is written, and §8.2's numbers only name topics. Where ADRs 0005 and 0006 and the plan cite 0014 to 0019, read the topic, not the number. CLAUDE.md and the ROADMAP cited two of them for ADRs not yet written ("ADR 0014", the offline wait; "ADR 0015", audio renders), numbers the next ADRs written will take for other topics; this step names those two by topic instead (the CLAUDE.md lines wait for your approval), and each gets its number when it is written.

## Decision

### 1. Determinism: integers and a counter RNG (`core/rng`, `core/util`)
- **No floating point in any decision or timing.** Ticks at 480 PPQ (a 16th is 120), permille for swing and shares, `p16` probabilities in [0, 65536], integer velocities. Percentages in content convert to `p16` rounding half up. The swing delay of an odd 16th is `floor(((sw − 500)·240 + 500) / 1000)` ticks.
- **Counter-based RNG**, exactly plan §4.2: `mix64` (SplitMix64's output function), `Key.with(x) = {mix64(h ^ x)}`, `below` by Lemire's multiply-shift (no rejection loop). `tag(name)` is FNV-1a 64 over UTF-8. A song's root is `seedKey(seed).with(tag(kitId))`, so one seed gives unrelated songs in different kits; each stage, lane, pattern, attempt, bar and step hangs below it on its own tag. **Tag strings and the root are permanent once released**: renaming a tag changes every draw below it.
- One key per decision: `chance` and `below` read the same bits for the same key.
- Never `std::*_distribution`, never iteration over unordered containers, `std::stable_sort` with a total order, `floorDiv` for negative offsets, a fixed attempt cap on every loop (16 for rhythm patterns, then the nearest attempt).

### 2. Four directions as data, one engine (`core/gen`, `content/`)
- **Three integer-only JSON files per kit:** `content/kits/<kit>.json` (tempo, key and Camelot code, base swing, lanes: track names, MIDI channels, drum notes or note ranges), `content/styles/<style>.json` (the form, the harmony grammar, each lane's rhythm grids), `content/feels/<style>.json` (per-lane feel and a row per section kind). FUT01 uses `garage2step`, FUT02 `sciclub`, FUT03 `futurehouse`, FUT04 `techno`.
- **A strict reader, no JSON library** (D3): integers and strings only. It refuses fractions, exponents, leading zeros, −0, overflow, duplicate or unknown keys, trailing commas, comments, a BOM, UTF-16 text (by its byte order mark or a NUL in its first two bytes, as Windows PowerShell 5.1 and Notepad's "Unicode" save it, named as such), ill-formed UTF-8 (RFC 3629) and nesting deeper than 64, and names the file, path, line and column.
- **Genre conventions live in the data,** as probability grids with anchors (a 100% cell), syncopation bands (LHL) on the grids that set one (two so far: the garage and house hats), Euclidean and polymeter cells, "never" patterns (2-step's kick never plays four on the floor), basslines that read a kick lane (leaving its steps, or following it where a cell says), harmony with modes, loops, harmonic rhythms of one to three chords, pushed changes and an anti-cliché memory, and voicing palettes per section kind. Engine features one style needs are opt-in fields that the others leave out: the arp lane, CC lanes, the kick duck, held pads, per-step slides and loop mutation (acid), "tight" notes that keep their written timing, pedal (one-chord) harmony, and a drum grid's build ladder (below).
- **Builds build.** A drum grid may be a ladder, `"stages"`: the section split into equal parts, each playing one of the lane's grids (every style's snare goes quarters, then 8ths, then 16ths), and a `"ramp"`, a velocity crescendo from the section's first bar to its last (65% to 110% on the snare). The hats open up (garage) or fill in (the four-on-the-floor styles) in the last quarter, and every lane with a filter (CC74) sweeps up through the build; a drop or drop 2 starts at least as open as the build before it ends. A ladder is one level deep and only the grid a section kind uses ramps; the loader refuses the rest.
- **Harmony agrees across lanes.** The chord lanes are scored before the bass. The bass's third, seventh and fifth agree with the pitch classes the chords voice over the same chord: a bass tone they don't voice may not sit a semitone under one they do (a minor 9th over the bass), nor be another third, seventh or fifth than theirs, which planed shapes, sus and quartal stacks don't voice; it falls back to the fifth, then the root, and a note held into the next chord that would rub against it ends at the change. The bass's seventh is the minor one: where the mode's 7th is major, it plays the fifth. No stack puts a b9 on its own root: a scale step a minor 9th (or 2nd) over the root moves up to the 11th (or 4th), so m9 and add9 become m11 and add11 there, and sus2 sus4. Planing keeps its out-of-key colour on major chords in the garage and house styles; the sci-fi club style, whose arp climbs the mode's own chord tones, keeps its quartal stacks to minor chords. A voicing's `top` range is a hard window for its top voice whenever some placement fits it, re-voicings included, so voice leading can't walk a stack down into the sub's register.
- **No mud in the stacks.** Every placement keeps the style's `mud` rules (`voicingCandidates`): below MIDI 52 neighbouring voices at least 5 semitones apart, no two voices a semitone apart with the lower one under 64 (E4), and none a minor ninth apart with the lower one under 60 (C4), the low rubs the kits' own written voicings never hold (before the rule, up to 44% of a section's chord-sounding time held one: FUT04's break drone). Whole-step seconds stay: they are the palette's colour (m7, add9, close sus2), for the S1d listen to judge. The minor-ninth rule generalises the b9 rule above to any two voices.
- **Harmonic rhythms hold at most three chords** (the plan's two or three, or `[4]` for a pedal). A progression's weight is a product of up to seven weights of at most 100 each, times the penalty's 1000: at most 1e17, and the 343 candidates sum to at most 3.4e16, far inside 64 bits. A fourth chord could reach 1e18 before the penalty and overflow; the loader refuses it.
- **Reserved for S1b2** (plan §4.1's rhythm stage, not built in part 1): syncopation bands per energy level, and the displacement and ratchet operators. A section's `energy` is parsed and range-checked but read by nothing yet. Also, from part 1's reviews, one line each:
  - FUT01's reese drop layer (plan §2.3: long notes on 1, b2, 5, b7 with slides and "talk" moves); its bass track is already named "Sub + Reese".
  - A per-seed motif for FUT02's arp: its cells come from a short list per grid, so 2-bar lead motifs recur across seeds (R10 lead 8.4% on 64 seeds, against a band of at most 3%).
  - Lanes moving inside the drops: every drop and drop2 block lists every lane for all its bars, and FUT01's drop is no denser than its groove (50.8 against 50.7 notes a bar, on 64 seeds). The form grammar's moves on 8- and 16-bar lines (a lane out for 8 bars, a one-bar dropout before the 16-bar line, drop 2 adding a layer drop 1 lacks), tested as at least one lane change inside every drop and drop 1 at least 10% denser than the groove.
  - Drop and break starts drawn on the 8-bar grid, about 70% on 16-bar lines and 30% on 8 mod 16 (as real club tracks place them, measured on 1,020 annotated four-on-the-floor tracks), builds of 8 to 32 bars and 200 to 240 bars in all, the 32-bar intro and outro kept; tested on 64 seeds per style.
- **No JSON Schema for styles and feels yet:** the field comments on the structs in `core/gen/content.h` document them, and the loaders enforce them.
- **Drums on channel 10, all inside 35–59** (plan §2.6), so exported MIDI plays the kit's drums when it comes back in. The kick, snare, clap, rim, hats and ride sit on their GM notes. GM has no shaker inside 35–59, so the shaker lanes use 54, GM's Tambourine, and the perc lanes use GM's toms (45, 47, 48, 50) and Cowbell (56): a GM kit plays them as those, the kit's own sounds as themselves.

### 3. Feel: score and performance kept apart (`core/gen/feel`)
- Every lane keeps its score (the quantized version) and its performance (the version with feel); the export writes both.
- The feel, always the last stage: per-lane swing relative to the kit's, 1/f timing and velocity (Voss–McCartney, 7 rows plus a white row, never i.i.d. jitter), anchors within ±2 ticks, the low-end lock (a bass onset on a kick step takes the kick's performed tick), "after" rules (a clap a few ticks behind its snare), chord spread up to 4 ticks, a velocity dip before accents, articulation and the amount knob.
- **A row only overrides.** Each feel lane sets its swing, sd and clamp at its base, which every section kind falls back to; the loader refuses a lane that sets one only in a row. A lock is set at the lane's base too, never in a row, and its target locks to nothing (one level, so no chain or cycle), because the feel orders the lanes by their locks and reads every target from the base.
- **No note crosses a section boundary**, in either version, whatever the feel's values: the feel's last pass clamps every onset into its section and ends every note by the section's end (tested at the loader's extremes: a kit swing of 750, +250 on a lane, a clamp of 60, an after rule of 30 and the amount at 2000). That is what lets the regions tile the song exactly. Rules that look at neighbouring notes stay inside a section too, so a region is the same whatever region follows it. The bass's hold gap, for one, leaves room only before a next note in the note's own active range, so a held note that reaches its section's end ends the same whether or not the next section starts with a bass note on the line. Only a lane without a duck can show it, since a duck ends every note its gap before its section's end anyway (44 to 60 ticks, more than any lane's hold gap): FUT04's rumble has a note on six of the seven section lines of every song, but with the rule or without, its duck ends the note before each at least 60 ticks short of the line, while FUT01's and FUT03's bass, the lanes without a duck, start no section on its line. So the rule changes no shipped output, and a test edits garage2step so its bass starts the break on the line. Inside a section, a note can sit a few ticks ahead of an 8-bar line, as played.
- **Anchors on the swung grid:** an anchor (a 100% cell) stays within the clamp of its place on the lane's swung grid, its 16th plus the lane's swing on an odd 16th (plan §2.3: the kick "500 on even 16ths, 580 on odd 16ths, ±2 ticks"), so a build's 16th roll swings like everything else.
- **The bass grooves against the kick** (round 1's binding rule, `docs/plans/S1-four-kits.md` and ADR 0010: it "leaves room on the kick and answers it in the gaps"). FUT01's line used to follow the 2-step kick, as plan §2.3's older "the sub follows the kick" and round 1's sub-hold fix had it: 55% of its drop onsets sat on a kick step, while the kit's own written Walk line puts none there, and `docs/listening.md` names following the kick as why the reese "doesnt make me want to dance". Round 2 resolved the conflict for round 1's rule: the sub now starts on the a of 1, holds through beat 2 and answers the kick at steps 6–8 and 12–15 (on 64 seeds, 1.4% of its drop onsets on a kick step, beat 2 never silent), and keeps that first note under the rare skip kick on the a of 1 (the lock puts it on the kick). It also takes FUT01's one G8 failure with it (64 of 64 seeds now). It is a one-file change in `content/styles/garage2step.json` (the old line is in its history); judge the two by ear and say if the old one should come back.
- **Open, for your decision (the S1b2 gating ADR): the lanes swing in tiers.** Round 1's binding rule says "every lane uses the kit's one swing" (`docs/plans/S1-four-kits.md`, from your note that the bass didn't make you want to dance); the plan's per-lane swing table and G8 (at least three distinct swings among the non-anchor lanes) predate it, and the feels follow the plan: in every kit the bass is the straightest lane and the hats, shaker and perc the most swung (FUT01's drop: bass 11 ticks, stabs 16, kick 17, perc 22, hats 26, shaker 33; FUT04's anvil perc a deep shuffle that makes its 3/16 polymeter alternate 340 and 380 ticks; these figures are round 1's). Moving every lane to the kit's swing makes G8 fail on all four kits (measured: 0 of 64 seeds keep three swings, on each), and playing polymeter cells straight breaks G7's perc microtiming on FUT01 and FUT04 (the residual SD rises to about 8 ticks, lag-1 below 0) and G8 on FUT04 (20 of 64 seeds keep three swings). Part 1 keeps the tiers until you choose: keep them and judge them at the S1d listen; or follow round 1 (the bass, chords and arp on the kit's swing, the hats within 2 ticks of it, polymeters straight) and replace G8 with "every lane within ±3 ticks of the kit's swing".

### 4. The extended mix, exported as regions (`core/gen/arrange`, `core/gen/export`)
- **Sections on 8-bar lines**, of kinds intro, groove, build, drop, break, drop2 and outro. Each style's form is an extended mix of 6 to 7½ minutes (FUT01 6:12, FUT02 6:53, FUT03 6:15, FUT04 7:03). The loader enforces the DJ rules: the form starts with a 32-bar intro whose first 8-bar block is drums only and ends with a 32-bar outro whose last block is drums only; in the intro lanes only join and in the outro they only leave, at least one every 8 or 16 bars (never three blocks in a row with the same lanes). Lanes join and leave per 8-bar block, in the data, and a lane listed in a block sounds in it (a break's sparse lanes have an anchor to make sure of it).
- **The drops are new material:** each section kind starts on its own pattern variant (drop 4, drop 2 8), so the first drop doesn't replay the groove's opening bars and the second doesn't replay the first.
- **Per seed and version** (`<out>/feel/`, `<out>/quantized/`):
  - the full arrangement, `<KIT>_seed<N>_<bpm>bpm_<camelot>_full.mid`: SMF type 1, 480 PPQ, a conductor track (name, tempo, 4/4, the key signature, a marker FF 06 `drop (04, 32 bars)` at every section start, the kind first), then one named track per lane;
  - one region per section, `regions/NN_<kind>_<bars>bars.mid`, all lanes, ending on its bar line;
  - `regions.json`: each region's file, kind, start bar (0-based `startBar` and the DAW's `dawBar`, counting from 1), length, lanes, and lanes per 8-bar block (each block with its `startBar` and `dawBar` too).
- **A `--bars` cut is the whole song's head** (`groovecore gen --bars N`, N a multiple of 8; `core/gen/cut.h`). The generator always makes the whole form, then cuts the finished song at bar N. The cut keeps every score note that starts before bar N, with its performed note, both ended by the cut (a performed onset at or after it, which only the feel's extremes produce, plays on the cut's last tick, as at a section's end); the controllers, turnarounds, salient changes and chord spans before it (a span that crosses it ended by it); and the sections that start before it, the last one shortened with its blocks. So the files hold the whole song's head by the score: the quantized full file is exactly the whole song's first N bars (a note that crosses the cut ended at it), and the feel file holds the performances of their notes, so a note on bar N's line that is played a few ticks early, inside the first N bars by time, is left out with its score note. The regions are the whole song's, the last one shortened: its file name, marker and `regions.json` entry (bars, ticks, blocks and lanes) give the new length, and `regions.json`'s `bars` and `length` the cut's. The cut used to be made first, on a truncated form, with stages, ramps, CC arcs and salient changes laid out on each section's planned length to fake a prefix; an in-section cut still differed near its end (the bass's approach into a chord change pushed onto the line, and its agreement with stabs pushed into the next chord, changed notes up to 960 ticks before it; in all, 738 of 1,680 cuts on 16 seeds per kit were not the whole song's head). Those planned lengths are gone: every stage runs on the whole form, whose sections have their whole length, so full songs are unchanged byte for byte (every snapshot hash, and 64 seeds of every kit against the build before the change), and so are cuts on section lines. Tested at every 8-bar line of 16 seeds of every kit, at the feel's extremes, and through `groovecore` itself (`cli/tests/cut.cmake`: FUT01 seed 31 cut 8 bars into its second drop, every file checked against the whole song's by `groovecore_cut_check`). The checker reads the files alone, which don't say which score note a performed note plays, so it pairs them key by key in time order inside the section the cut ends, the only notes whose pairing matters. That is exact for the shipped feels at every 8-bar line (64 seeds of every kit); at the feel's extremes a note can play after a later one, or two on one tick, which the file merges, and there it says what the files can't tell (exit 3) instead of failing a right cut (three more CLI tests).
- **One `--out` folder per song**, both versions. Region names carry no seed and every seed of a kit has the same ones, so `writeFiles` refuses, before writing anything, a folder whose `feel/` or `quantized/` holds another song's full file (another `*_full.mid`) or a `regions.json` of another kit or seed (or one it can't read), and a `regions/` folder being written that holds a region file this song doesn't write (another song's, or the same seed cut with `--bars`). It never deletes a file.
- The MIDI writer (`core/midi`): a fixed event order on a tick (meta, note-offs, program changes, controllers, note-ons), no running status, every track ending on the song's end tick, release velocity 64, and refusals with a message for out-of-range values, invalid UTF-8, two values for one thing on one tick and overlapping same-key notes. The reader carries running status across meta and sysex events, pairs note-offs first in, first out, and refuses format 2 and SMPTE timing.

### 5. Metrics: definitions, tolerances and the "test of the test" (`tools/legacy_audit`, `core/metrics`)
- `tools/legacy_audit/README.md` defines every gating metric (G1–G10), reported metric (R1–R11) and invariant, from the MIDI bytes alone, and names the version each reads (quantized for what is written, feel for how it is played). The Node audit's output on the 516-file baseline is `core/tests/legacy_expected.json`, never regenerated to make a test pass.
- **The C++ port must match it exactly**, except DFA alpha, ±1 in thousandths (it never needed it: all 5,842 values match). Doubles appear only in the SD and DFA alpha, in the README's operation order; lag-1 is exact through a portable 128-bit integer; a JavaScript null is −1, an undefined lag-1 or alpha −9999.
- **Definitions fixed where the planning audits disagreed:** the hat accent period counts closed hats only (867‰ on the drum parts, against 76.5% or 81%); G4 compares a bar with the bar one phrase (8 bars) earlier and counts a run of changes once; G2 reads chord changes at half-bar resolution (26 of 43 legacy kits, not the plan's 43 of 43).
- **The bands of G3, G5, G6 and G7 are pure functions** of each gate's aggregate, alike in the audit (`metrics.js`, `bands`) and the C++ (`core/metrics`, `bands::`), and `tools/legacy_audit/fixtures/expected.json` ("bands") pins every bound at its edge for both: the bound itself passes, one unit past it fails. The gates' fixtures fail on more than one part of a band, and the generator's data sits away from the edges, so neither pinned a bound.
- **`--check` compares every section both ways:** a section the expected file has and the result doesn't, or the other way round, is a difference; only the expected file's header (the Node, manifest and core.js it records) and the result's `about` and `format` are left out.
- **Open for the S1b2 gating ADR:** whether G4 gets a ceiling (legacy fails it only through the ExtendedMix lead); whether G7 is measured per track rather than per drum group (FUT01's and FUT03's hats fail only because the group merges the hats with an independently fluctuating shaker); an exemption or another measure for one-chord pedal styles in G1 and G2 (FUT04 fails them by design); G5's ghost group for styles without ghost notes (FUT04's sparse snare has none, so G5 finds no ghost windows); G8 with the swing tiers (§3; all four kits pass it since FUT01's bass stopped following the kick); and G9's rarity rule: a perc key that sounds in most of a section's bars (a polymeter or Euclid cycle) shouldn't make a fill, while snare-family notes and occasional perc still count (FUT01's 3/16 rim makes most of its G9 fills: in round 2's review, 457 fills on seeds 11–30 dropped to 63 without the rim). S1b2 measures its fills against that rule; changing it changes legacy_expected.json's G9 values, so it needs your OK, an ADR note and a commit of its own.
- `groovecore metrics` scores either layout: the legacy one (`<KIT>/<KIT>_<slot>.mid`, each gate on its legacy file sets, its primary set deciding) or seeds (a `feel/` file and its `quantized/` twin are one seed; `regions/` is skipped).

### 6. The legacy baseline and the Node pin (`tools/export_golden.js`, `cmake/WubansiaGolden.cmake`)
- `node tools/export_golden.js` runs frozen `core.js` and writes its 12 MIDI files per kit (`kitMidiFiles`, what the web app's "Save all kits" writes) for the 43 kits, at their own key and BPM: 516 files, `<KIT>/<KIT>_<slot>.mid`, 903,129 notes, 8,365,094 bytes. Nothing is committed but the manifest, `content/golden/legacy-v21.sha256`, whose header names the Node version, core.js's SHA-256 and the file count.
- **Node is pinned in `.nvmrc` (22.22.0)**, because core.js writes `Math.sin` and `Math.pow` results into the bytes. The exporter and the audit refuse any other version (exit 2), CI installs it with `actions/setup-node` from `.nvmrc`, and CMake reports a mismatch at configure time and skips the Node tests. Measured: Node 20.20.0, 21.7.3 and Bun 1.4.4 give the same bytes, and moving every libm result by up to 256 ulps changes none, so the pin is a margin.
- **`golden_check`** exports, checks the files against the manifest byte for byte (CMake's own SHA-256, sharing no code with the exporter; the checker takes relative paths from the folder it runs in), then runs `groovecore metrics --legacy --expect-fail --check core/tests/legacy_expected.json`: it passes only if legacy fails every gating band on its primary set and every value equals the committed expectation (DFA alpha within ±1 in thousandths, §5).
- CI runs `golden_check` before the tests, so the C++ legacy-metric tests read the fresh export, and no longer ignores `legacy/**`: a change to core.js runs the check (plan §3.4).

### 7. Generator snapshots, not goldens (`core/tests/snapshots/gen.sha256`)
- The SHA-256 of **every file `groovecore gen` writes** for 16 seeds (0–10, 42, 977, 65536, 123456789, 2^64 − 1) × {feel, quantized} × the four kits: 1,312 files, by path under `--out <KIT>/seed<N>`.
- Checked twice: in memory by `core_tests` (left universal on macOS, so the x86_64 slice can run under Rosetta) and through the CLI by `cli/tests/snapshots.cmake`, files on disk included.
- They change only in their own commit, with the reason, until they are frozen in S1h (CLAUDE.md). The test writes the fresh manifest into the build tree and says how to accept it.

## Consequences
- On 64 seeds per style, part 1 passes 4 to 8 of the 10 gates (FUT01 5, FUT02 8, FUT03 5, FUT04 4); legacy fails each gate on its primary set. G9 (fills) and G10 (form variety) fail everywhere, and G4 and G5 wherever there is no lead lane: those are S1b2's form grammar, hook, motifs and fills. G7 on FUT01/FUT03 and G1/G2 on FUT04 need the decisions in §5. `core/tests/test_gen_gates.cpp` runs the metric suite on the generator's own 64 seeds per style and pins these verdicts, so a regression fails ctest, and S1b2 updates the list as it fixes a gate.
- Kick and bass onsets are within 2 ticks in 100% of pairs, every section starts on an 8-bar line, kick anchors stay within ±2 ticks, and 128 bars take about 4 to 5 ms in Release (both versions' files included, `core_tests [timing]`), against a 50 ms budget; a 128-bar cut, which makes the whole song first, about 5 to 6 ms.
- Byte identity is proven on Linux x86_64 with clang 18 and GCC 13, and on macOS arm64 with AppleClang 17 (Xcode 16.4), where the macOS CI job ran both snapshot tests and passed. That job runs both snapshot tests on arm64 on every ready (non-draft) pull request to main, S1b1's included, so S1b1's own PR gates on arm64 parity, and a mismatch there is a determinism bug to fix in the code, never in the manifest. S1b2's dispatched checkpoint adds the x86_64 slice under Rosetta.
- `golden.export_golden` and `golden.legacy_audit` add about 40 s to a Linux ctest run.
- Every content or generator change that alters output regenerates the snapshots in a commit of its own.

## Alternatives
- **A JSON library** (nlohmann/json): declined in D3; the integer-only reader is small and refuses floats by construction.
- **`std::mt19937` or `<random>` distributions:** sequential state (adding a lane shifts every later draw) and distributions that differ between libc++ and libstdc++.
- **Region files named with the seed** (`FUT01_seed7_03_drop_32bars.mid`): the brief names them `03_drop_32bars.mid`, and the folder carries the seed. Refusing a mixed folder keeps the names short and the folders honest.
- **Snapshots of the full files only** (the plan's 16 seeds × 2): the region files and `regions.json` are what a producer drags in, so they are hashed too.
- **A tolerance on golden_check's metrics:** the expected values are integers by design; only DFA alpha has a stated tolerance.
