# 0019. MIDI v2: one channel map, musical roots, the loops and extended mixes as the plugin plays them

**Status:** Proposed, 2026-11-05. Part of the plugin v0.1 plan (MIDI v2, package P1); for the user's sign-off before it reaches main, with every content/midi file it regenerates.

## Context
Wubansia v0.1 plays the committed MIDI files, and its drag strip hands them out: the files are the score. Two audits of the round 5 MIDI found the files valid but wrong in a sampler:
- **Correctness (audit A, 16 defects).** The chord sample's trigger shared channel 3 with the synth voicing, and every phrase sat on channel 4 with the extended mix's trigger, so one sample played another's notes. Chord samples rooted at D3, E3 and G3 were triggered on C4 (note 60), and FUT03's dub stab had two roots. Swung notes ran into the next one (52 overlaps in FUT01's mix, 272 in FUT04's), which a legato synth plays as slides nobody wrote. FUT03's fill bar was its groove bar, FUT02's hats played through the fill's own, no file had a key signature, and the cues (riser, fills, vox) were labels that played nothing.
- **Musicality (audit B).** The drop was the groove, nothing changed inside a section, the hook and the ear candy weren't notes, the harmony never moved, every build was the same, and the DJ ends were 16 bars.

## Decision
- **One channel per instrument role, the same in every kit** (`tools/sound/channels.py`, `docs/midi-map.md`): 1 bass, 2 lead, 3 chord voicing (Wubansia ignores it), 4 chords, 5 vox synth, 6 accents, 7 FX and 8 vox chops (one key a sound from 48), 9 reserved, 10 drums (35-59), 11-16 each kit's extras (FUT01's organ bass, organ lead and air pad; FUT02's drop glide, laser chord stab and glass ping; FUT03's warm pad; FUT04's acid stab, metal chord and rave stab), each extra on a plugin lane. FUT04's rumble is drum note 35, on its own track. The engine keeps a C++ copy of the table.
- **Musical roots.** Every pitched sample is triggered at its root (no more note 60): the chord files are rooted where the recipes rooted them (FUT01 54/59, FUT02 50, FUT03 55 for Gm9 and 60 for Cm9, FUT04 52).
- **The files.** `<KIT>_Loop_Vox_4bars.mid` and `<KIT>_Loop_NoVox_4bars.mid` replace FullLoop: each kit's round 5 context loop lane by lane, its accents, FX, extras, rides and throws included. The part files stay on their channels: `Chords_<Sample>` (was SampleTrigger) and `Chords_<Sample>_Voicing`; new `Accents_Vox`, `Accents_NoVox` and `FX`; the phrases named by phrase. `extended/vox/` and `extended/novox/` each hold the 224-bar mix, its regions and an integer-only `regions.json`.
- **Timing.** 960 PPQ, the swing written in; on a tick, note-offs, then controllers, then note-ons; no same-key overlaps; tracks end on bar lines. Swing never runs a note into the next one on its track, and a mono line (bass, vox) never runs into a note it doesn't slide to; a written slide holds the note before 12 ticks into it. The tempo meta is the integer microseconds nearest the BPM (128 is exact; 130, 134 and 136 BPM read at most 1.1 parts per million fast, 0.42 ms over the mix). Every file carries its key (FF 59).
- **Velocities pick layers.** Wubansia's sampler plays the layer nearest a velocity by |velocity - round(layer x 127)|, the louder on a tie. A velocity is written round(v x 127), unless that plays another layer than the recipe picks from v (or reading velocity / 127 would): then the nearest integer that plays the recipe's layer both ways (FUT01's 0.9 kicks, halfway between two layers, are 115, not 114; FUT01's and FUT03's 0.3 ghost claps are 37, not 38).
- **End-aligned sounds end on their target**, starting the sample's length before it; PARTS carries the lengths (48 kHz frames), which a test checks against the WAVs.
- **Controllers.** CC74 (cutoff) and CC71 (resonance) every 60 ticks while a filter rides, on one law with the mix manifests (`channels.cc_value`): FUT01's bass wobble (650-2210 Hz), FUT02's arp (1.6-16 kHz), FUT03's dub stab (900-5000 Hz), FUT04's acid ride (x1-x2.6 over 5400-14040 Hz, and its resonance 0.90-0.96). CC91 and CC94 throw a note: 127 on its tick before its note-on, 0 at its note-off or at the next note-on on its track that isn't thrown, whichever comes first (Wubansia latches the sends at each note-on); every track on a channel that throws anywhere in a kit's files starts with both at 0 on tick 0.
- **The extended mix as data.** Each kit declares its music in PARTS (`tools/sound/README.md`, "MIDI"), its arrangement as `form`. The machinery gives a form per-section basslines, vox modes (loop, riff, call, answer, throw), accent and FX rules (the loop's accents in the drops' blocks and every other groove block, lead-ins in the breaks, none in the drum-only ends, the and-of-4 stab, swells and uplifts into a line's end, impacts on the drops, risers and fills end-aligned on their cues), phrase lanes, chord blocks, a pad lane, kit-authored busy hats and turnarounds (with defaults), per-genre builds that roll up to the fill's start with velocities rising hit by hit, 32-bar drums-only ends, and drops that differ from grooves. The genres' default forms use all of it until each kit's engineer writes the kit's own.
- **Tests first,** in `tools/sound/tests/test_midi.py` (the kit-agnostic properties) and `test_fut0N_midi.py` (each kit's own: its context loop lane by lane, roots, phrase channels, rides, throws), so each kit's engineer edits only their kit's file.

## Consequences
- Every committed content/midi file is regenerated (its own commits). DAW setups built from the earlier MIDI need re-rooting: the chord samples now sit at their roots, the phrases left channel 4, and FullLoop and SampleTrigger are gone.
- Wubansia's MIDI input reads channels (Multi), with a one-lane mode for clips that drop them (Ableton's do).
- The manifests (P2, P3) read the same table and laws. FUT01's loop fill starts its file at its 16th 4, which the kit manifest carries as a start offset (`start_frames`).
- The kits' arrangements are defaults until the kit engineers write them: the audit's per-kit proposals are TODO hooks in each kit's `midi.py`.
- Not verified: nothing has been opened in a DAW or listened to. The routing notes come from the DAWs' documented behaviour; the arrangement rules are unheard.

## Alternatives
- **Keep note 60 for one-file chord samples:** Ableton's Simpler plays a sample at its own pitch on C3, but any player that knows a sample's root plays it 5-10 semitones off, and one sample can't have two roots.
- **One channel for everything but the drums** (a note map, plan section 2.6): the drum map collides with the basslines (FUT02's C2 would play the kick), so channels are kept.
- **Pitch bend for glides and dips:** truer to the recipes, but a second controller lane every player must follow; slides as legato overlaps (12 ticks) work in any mono synth.
- **Keep the cues as labels only:** they play nothing, and the vox, accents and FX are the kits' hooks; they are notes now, the cues kept as labels for the reader.
