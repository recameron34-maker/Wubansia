# v0.3 Creative toolkit: the build plan

Lead engineer's merge of the four v0.3 designs (`plan-part-drags.md`, `plan-drum-kit.md`, `plan-phrases.md`,
`plan-looks.md`, all four present and read in full), their critiques, and one new step, chords as chords. Base for
every step: `9d4c1b1` (branch `s1b2b`, pushed as `origin/claude/cool-allen-jvmith`), the finished For my synths step
(ADR 0027) with ADR 0028. Line references are at `9d4c1b1` unless they say otherwise. The worktree
`scratchpad/s1b2b` was only read. Every figure this plan adds was measured; section 9 lists each command.

## 0. For you, in short

v0.3 turns Wubansia into a set of tools for your own tracks. Nothing waits for a listening round or an approval: each
part goes in as an option, and you cut what you don't want (ADR 0028).

- **Chords on your synths.** With For my synths lit, a chord sample's track is written as the whole chord its sample
  plays, voiced as the kit voices it and sitting above the bass, instead of one root note per chord.
- **The Phrases drawer.** A Phrases button next to Samples opens a drawer over the lanes. It starts with each kit's own
  phrases and lead line (they move there from the drag strip), then fills with 64 new eight-bar phrases per kit,
  written over the kit's own chords in eight kinds (hooks, call and answer, periods, sparse lines, arps, stabs,
  counter-lines, bounce). Click one to hear it with the groove, drag it to your DAW, ask for 8 more, star the ones you
  like.
- **Part drags.** A Part choice on the drag strip (All, Drums, Bass, Chords, Lead, Vox, Accents) makes Loop and Song
  bring only that part. Pick Drums, light 1 drum track and drag Song: the whole 224-bar mix's drums arrive as one
  track, ready for your own drum samples.
- **Drum kits.** A Drum kit button saves the playing kit's drums into your samples folder: a WAV per pad for a Drum
  Rack or Drum Machine Designer, every layer and round robin, an SFZ file that plays the drum MIDI as Wubansia does,
  and a README with the pad map.
- **Four looks.** Each kit wears the look you chose: FUT01 night bus, FUT02 mission HUD, FUT03 deep glass, FUT04
  machine room. The look switches with the kit, every control stays in the same place, and every light shows
  something real (the kit's own MIDI, its chord, its levels, the plugin's output).

Not checked anywhere in v0.3: how any of it sounds or behaves in Live, Logic or another DAW, on Windows, or at Retina
scale. Each step says what it couldn't verify.

**Why this order.**
- `chords` goes first: it is small, the user asked for it with For my synths, and it collides least there. It owns
  `core/midi/for_synths`, the manifest's new `voicing` key and the For my synths tooltip and wording. The only step
  beside it, `drawer`, touches the same files (`DragFiles.cpp`, `DragChip.cpp`, `dist/INSTALL.md`'s drag section,
  `test_install_synths.py`) only in other hunks. Run later, it would have to re-thread the transform through Part,
  phrase and drum kit code.
- `drawer` is the critique's "land the strip change first" (accepted): the phrase chips leave the drag strip in a
  small step, without waiting for the phrase generator and the real-time overlay. Part and Drum kit need that room.
  Measured in `plan-part-drags.md` §4.2: with the chips there, only 152 px are left for FUT01's five chips, which need
  324.
- `parts` before `drumkit`: both edit `DragStrip` and its fit test, and Drum kit takes the 164 px Part leaves. One at
  a time, Part first, so the second rebases a finished layout.
- `phrases` is the long pole: the generator, the library, the CLI and the engine overlay come first (part A, core,
  CLI, content and engine, no plugin link). That pairs well with `parts`. Its plugin half (part B) follows once `parts`
  is in, because it reads `EditorModel::mutedLanes`, which `parts` adds.
- `looks` goes last: its commit 3 rewrites every editor file through a skin. Run while the other UI steps were still
  editing those files, it would conflict everywhere. Its fonts, pattern view and scope commits (2, 4, 5) touch no
  sibling's lines, so they may start once `drumkit` is in, while `phrases` finishes.

**Two engineers at most (4 CPUs).** Measured (§9, command 9): one full release build costs 1,264 s of CPU (21.1 min,
at least 5.3 min on 4 cores), 884 s of it under `plugin/`. Every plugin change relinks four large targets:
`WubansiaPluginTests` 87.4 s, the Standalone 66.5 s, the VST3 60.4 s and the CLAP 59.8 s. Two engineers building the
plugin at once each get about 2 cores, which doubles each one's edit, build and test cycle. So:

- **Worth pairing:** a plugin-heavy step with a step whose early commits build only `core_tests`, `engine_tests` or
  `groovecore`. That gives `chords` ‖ `drawer` (chords' commits 1-4 are core, engine and Python) and `parts` ‖
  `phrases` part A (commits 1-8: core, CLI, content and engine).
- **Pairable but slower:** `drumkit` ‖ `phrases` part B, both plugin-heavy. Do it to keep the calendar short, and
  build only the target under test while iterating
  (`cmake --build --preset release --target WubansiaPluginTests`, about 87 s of link instead of about 270 s).
- **Not worth pairing:** `looks` with anything but the tail of `phrases` (it touches every editor file), and `close`.
- Each engineer runs `cmake --workflow --preset release` and `tools/ci/validate.sh build/release` once before handing
  over, not after every commit.

**Branches, worktrees and merges.**
- Integration branch `v03`, made by `plan` at `9d4c1b1` (the main checkout's local `claude/cool-allen-jvmith` is at
  `216e1af`, 713 commits behind: never branch from it). Integration worktree `$S/v03-int`, where
  the lead's scratch folder `$S` (not in git) (`$S/v03/` holds these
  plans and briefs and is not a worktree).
- Each step: `git -C /home/user/Wubansia worktree add $S/v03-<step> -b v03-<step> v03`, then
  `git submodule update --init --recursive` inside it. It reuses the main repo's submodule objects, so nothing is
  downloaded.
- One change per commit; tests first; snapshots and generated files alone in their own commit.
- Merge back: the engineer rebases their own unpushed branch onto the current `v03` head and re-runs the checks; the
  lead does the one lean review (ADR 0028 decision 7), then `git -C $S/v03-int merge --no-ff v03-<step>`, and removes the
  step's worktree. The lead pushes and opens one PR per step; engineers commit only, never push.
- Merge order inside a wave: the smaller step first (`chords` before `drawer`, `parts` before `phrases`, `drumkit`
  before `phrases` part B if it is ready first). The second rebases.

## 4. Chords as chords: which kit data gives the exact chord (measured)

The step `chords` needs, for every note on a chord-sample track, the exact chord its sample plays. The truth is the
recipes' voicings, which the plugin doesn't carry: FUT01 Organ and E Piano Stab, root + (0, 7, 10, 14, 15)
(`tools/sound/recipes/fut01/keys.py:23-24`); Air Pad (54, 64, 69, 73, 80) (`fut01/music.py:30`); FUT02 Starfield Pad
(50, 57, 60, 65, 69, 76) (`fut02/pad.py:31`); Laser Chord Stab, B-flat maj9 on A#3 and Dm9 on D4
(`fut02/accents.py:64`); FUT03 Dub Stab and Pad Warm, (55, 62, 65, 69, 70) (`fut03/chords.py:28`, `:90-95`); FUT04
Drone (52, 59, 64, 67, 78) (`fut04/pad.py:30`); Metal Chord and Rave Stab, Em (64, 71, 76, 79) (`fut04/accents.py:52`).
A note on key K plays its zone's sample repitched by K − root, so it sounds K + the zone's intervals.

Over every chord-sample note of every kit's Loop and Song (vox and no-vox), its 4-bar Chords file and the five chord
phrase files, 3,301 notes in all (§9, command 1):

| Source | Exact chord | Wrong | None |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Chord voicing track, the notes starting on the note's tick in the same file | **2,171** (every Chords note in every Loop and Song, with the same length and velocity too) | 0 | 1,130: every note of the extras (Air Pad, Laser Chord Stab, Pad Warm, Metal Chord, Rave Stab), the 4-bar Chords files and the five phrase files |
| The instrument's name (kit.json) read as a chord symbol | 0 (a name never gives a voicing) | 646 notes whose repitched sample plays another chord than its name says (Air Pad F#m9 played as Bm9, the Metal Chord's Am and Bm) | 1,521 notes on instruments whose name holds no chord (Organ Stab, Laser Chord Stab, Pad Warm) |

So the Chord voicing track is the exact source, but it exists only beside the Chords track. The extras' chords are in
no plugin data at all. The step therefore carries each chord instrument's voicing in kit.json (`zones[].voicing`),
exported from the same recipe constants the voicing track was written from (`fut01/midi.py:124-126` writes it as
zone + `Ky.SHAPE`). A content test holds kit.json to the Chord voicing track at all 2,171 notes it witnesses, and
`tools/sound`'s tests hold it to the recipes.

**The register.** For my synths puts the bass at 38-55 (ADR 0027 decision 4); measured, its top key is 54 in FUT01
and 55 in FUT02-FUT04 (command 1). A chord is lifted whole octaves until its lowest note is 56 or higher (one above

## 6. What every step has in common

- **Checks before handing over** (CLAUDE.md "How to work"): `cmake --workflow --preset release` (configure, build,
  ctest); `cmake --build --preset release --target golden_check` (unchanged by every v0.3 step); for plugin changes
  `tools/ci/validate.sh build/release` (pluginval 1.0.4 at strictness 10; Linux needs `xvfb-run`, which the script
  handles); the Python suites the step touches (`python3 -m unittest discover -s tools/content/tests -t .`,
  `python3 -m unittest discover -s tools/sound/tests -t .` in a venv with `tools/sound/requirements.txt`); for UI, the
  screenshots `WUBANSIA_SCREENSHOTS=<dir> build/release/plugin/WubansiaPluginTests "[.screenshot]"`; for the audio or
  service threads, the local TSan subset (`docs/plans/v0.2-samples-spec.md:1048-1058`); with real content,
  `WUBANSIA_CONTENT_DIR=$S/real-content build/release/plugin/WubansiaPluginTests "[.real]"`.
- **Words:** each brief gives every on-screen word and tooltip. Artist names never appear in names, files or
  strings; new names pass the three denylists.
- **Determinism:** the same inputs give the same bytes on every platform; generated content and goldens change only
  in their own commit; `std::stable_sort`; integers where the plans say integers.
- **Audio thread:** no allocation, lock, log, file or throw in `processBlock` and what it calls. Only `phrases` (the
  overlay) and `looks` (the scope tap) touch it, and both add `[realtime]` cases.
- **Say what wasn't verified:** every handoff names what it couldn't check (a DAW, macOS rendering, Windows).
